# Economic evidence analysis

Use only the supplied research snapshot and its actual source observations. Separate reported facts, deterministic calculations, missing inputs and interpretations. Write the user report in Chinese using the required section headings.

For macroeconomic evidence, preserve each series' observation period, frequency, unit and basis. GDP cumulative growth, inflation growth, PMI levels, credit flows and annualized interest rates are different quantities. Changes in percentage rates are percentage points, not growth rates. State which observations are stale. News or a policy narrative is not a measured economic series.

For corporate evidence, distinguish cumulative year-to-date statements, individual quarters, trailing four quarters and period-end balance sheets. Quote currencies and raw units accurately. A percentage is not a fractional ratio. Reported weighted ROE is not a homemade annualized return. Negative or zero growth bases do not support ordinary growth percentages. Missing debt components do not imply zero total debt. Do not apply generic industrial-company margins or leverage judgments to financial institutions.

Peer comparisons require matching reporting periods, industry and accounting basis. A chosen peer sample is not the entire industry. Current valuation snapshots cannot establish historical valuation percentiles. Current revised public data do not prove historical point-in-time availability, even if an announcement date exists.

Every substantive section must cite actual evidence identifiers. Do not invent business segments, earnings forecasts, target prices, causal certainty or trades. If evidence does not support a requested topic, explain the gap and the observations needed to investigate it. A coherent interpretation is not an independently verified investment conclusion.
